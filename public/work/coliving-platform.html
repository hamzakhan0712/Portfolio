The problem

The operator rents beds, not flats. One room can hold several tenants, a free bed and a different rent for each, and every bed has its own move-in date, deposit and agreement end date.

Spreadsheets and WhatsApp could not answer the questions that mattered each morning: which beds are free, who has paid, and which agreements are about to end.

What I built

One Django application built around beds, with three kinds of users:

  • Staff manage properties, rooms and beds, send invitations, record rent, handle support tickets and work a leads pipeline, each limited by their role.
  • Tenants see their agreement, amount due, total paid and next rent date.
  • Landlords see only their own buildings, tenants, free beds and rent collected.
  • Every bed tracked on its own: occupied, free, reserved or under repair
  • Rent, contracts and repair requests recorded in one place
  • Separate logins for the operator, tenants and landlords

Backend decisions

The bed is the unit of rent
Property, room and bed are separate models. Rent, deposit and status sit on BedSpace, a room's occupancy is worked out from its beds, and room numbers are unique per property. Pricing a shared room bed by bed needed no special cases.
Contract changes are all or nothing
Before a tenant is assigned, the model checks that neither the bed nor the tenant already has an active contract. Creating or ending the contract and updating the bed's status then happen inside one transaction.atomic block, so a failure leaves no half-updated records.
Invitations hold a specific bed
Staff invite a prospect to one free bed. The invitation carries a UUID token that expires after seven days by default, and the bed is held as reserved so it cannot be offered twice. The prospect fills in their own details, and approval creates their login.
Permissions by module and action
Staff views go through decorators that call has_crud_permission(user, module, action), so each role gets create, read, update and delete rights per module. Over 200 checks across the codebase use the same function. Tenant and landlord portals are limited to the user's own records.
Routine work runs as commands
Management commands generate next month's rent from recurring schedules a week before it is due, send reminders and mark overdue payments. They run daily from a scheduler and support --dry-run, so a change can be checked before it touches real data.
Data in and out
Payments support 15 types, such as rent, deposit and fees, across methods from UPI to cheque. Properties import from Excel, and properties, tenants, payments, leads and tickets export to CSV or Excel.
